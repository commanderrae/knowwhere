This Day in History - April 17th

  • 1521 — Martin Luther refuses to recant before the Diet of Worms, declaring he cannot go against his conscience — a defining moment of the Protestant Reformation. His refusal would reshape Christianity and European politics for centuries. [Religion]
  • 1524 — Italian explorer Giovanni da Verrazzano becomes the first European navigator to enter New York Harbor, sailing past the future site of Manhattan Island and into the narrows that now bear his name. [Exploration]
  • 1790 — American Founding Father Benjamin Franklin dies in Philadelphia at the age of 84. Author, inventor, diplomat, and one of the principal architects of the United States, he was one of the most celebrated figures of the Enlightenment. [U.S. History]
  • 1895 — The Treaty of Shimonoseki is signed, ending the First Sino-Japanese War. China ceded Taiwan, the Penghu Islands, and the Liaodong Peninsula to Japan — a seismic shift in Asian power that would reverberate for decades. [War]
  • 1964 — Jerrie Mock becomes the first woman to complete a solo flight around the world, landing her single-engine Cessna 180 — nicknamed Spirit of Columbus — back in Columbus, Ohio after a 29-day journey covering 23,000 miles. [Aviation]
  • 1970 — The crew of Apollo 13 safely splashes down in the Pacific Ocean, four days after an oxygen tank explosion crippled the spacecraft en route to the Moon. NASA's improvised rescue effort is widely regarded as one of the greatest feats of engineering problem-solving in history. [Space]
  • 1975 — The Khmer Rouge under Pol Pot seizes Phnom Penh, beginning one of the most brutal regimes of the 20th century. The subsequent genocide would claim the lives of an estimated 1.5 to 2 million people — nearly a quarter of Cambodia's population. [Tragedy]
  • 1980 — Zimbabwe officially becomes an independent republic, with Robert Mugabe as its first Prime Minister. The date marked the end of white-minority rule under the former Rhodesia and was celebrated as a milestone of decolonization. [Independence]
  • 2007 — The Virginia Tech massacre occurs when a student opens fire across campus in Blacksburg, Virginia, killing 32 people and injuring 17 others. It remains the deadliest mass shooting by a single gunman in U.S. history. [Tragedy]
